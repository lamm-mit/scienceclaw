# HEA fatigue-crack-growth science investigation

## Scientific result

The open HEA fatigue-crack-growth summary supports a concrete fatigue-design hypothesis: threshold resistance and post-threshold crack-growth stability are decoupled.  A threshold-only model would rate dual-phase FCC+BCC records favorably because their median $\Delta K_{th}$ is 7.00 MPa sqrt(m), compared with 4.80 MPa sqrt(m) for FCC records.  The same FCC+BCC records have median Paris slope 5.90, compared with 3.16 for FCC records.

## Interpretation

The science-valued claim is that HEA fatigue design should not screen by threshold alone.  A two-axis map using both $\Delta K_{th}$ and Paris slope $m$ separates crack-initiation resistance from post-threshold growth stability and flags high-threshold/high-slope dual-phase records as potentially hazardous after threshold crossing.

## Limitations

This is a hypothesis from a literature-compiled open dataset, with small phase-group counts.  It is an actual materials-mechanics hypothesis, but it requires prospective validation before being treated as a general HEA design law.
